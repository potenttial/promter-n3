# The sargassum tech stack: how satellites, ocean models and drones now track the Mexican Caribbean's biggest risk

> NOAA's risk tool went daily at 1 km resolution in 2026, and UNAM reads Sentinel-2 imagery every five days. What the monitoring stack can and cannot do for hotels and tour operators.

Turisync Analysis · 2026-11-26 · Turisync

Sargassum is the single biggest operational risk for beach tourism in the Mexican Caribbean, and in 2026 the tools to anticipate it got sharper.

## The stack

| Layer | Who | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Regional risk | NOAA, Sargassum Inundation Risk (SIR) tool | Daily reports since 2026, about 1 km resolution (previously weekly, 5 km) |
| Local detection | UNAM's National Earth Observation Laboratory (LANOT) | Sentinel-2 images about every five days, Quintana Roo to Honduras |
| Forecasting | LANOT | Current, wind and wave models fill the gaps between images |
| Field validation | UNAM, with its Puerto Morelos marine unit | Floating GPS trackers, spectroradiometers, drones over reefs |
| Public signal | Quintana Roo government | Beach-by-beach traffic light |

LANOT holds an archive of about 4,700 images of the area for retrospective analysis, according to Gaceta UNAM.

## The scale of the problem
- About 40 million tonnes of sargassum biomass were expected in the Atlantic in 2026, more than in 2025.
- Quintana Roo received roughly 90,000 tonnes the previous year.
- Containment barriers cover about 90 km of coast and have not been enough, per LANOT's coordinator, Jorge Prado Molina.

## Where it stands now

On Nov. 11, 96 of 140 monitored beaches in Quintana Roo were at the low level. Winter is the low season for arrivals.

## Why it matters for operators

Daily, kilometre-scale risk data turns cleanup crews, beach staffing and guest communication into scheduled operations rather than emergency responses. The open question for the industry is integration: few hotels plug these feeds into their own systems.

## FAQ

**How is sargassum tracked in the Mexican Caribbean?**
With NOAA's daily Sargassum Inundation Risk tool, UNAM's analysis of Sentinel-2 satellite images every five days, ocean models and field data from GPS trackers and drones.

**How much sargassum reached Quintana Roo?**
Roughly 90,000 tonnes the previous year, according to UNAM's LANOT.

**Is there sargassum in the Mexican Caribbean now?**
Little. On Nov. 11, 2026, 96 of 140 monitored beaches were at the low level.
