The Mexican Caribbean's AI gap: hotels use chatbots, but few build their own systems

Only four in ten tourism companies in the region invest in technology, and a local tech director warns guests will notice the AI gap within 24 months. Where the opportunity is.

by Turisync Analysis · November 25, 2026 · 1 min read

The Mexican Caribbean runs one of the largest hotel markets in the Americas, but its technology investment lags its size.

The diagnosis

At a workshop of the Association of Vacation and Tourist Complexes (ACOTUR), supported by Anáhuac University, Jorge Carlos Castro Ortiz, technology director at Sapere, said the region is roughly level with the rest of Latin America but trails the U.S. and Europe significantly. He warned guests are likely to notice the gap within 24 months.

Participants agreed individuals use generative AI for emails and presentations, but companies have not yet built systems that solve business problems.

Where the opportunity is

  • Virtual concierges for routine guest questions.
  • Data cleaning across PMS, CRM and booking channels.
  • Personalized content for guests before and during the stay.
  • Recruitment and back-office automation, which Castro Ortiz described as fast and affordable.

For vendors, the pitch writes itself: a market with more than 100,000 hotel rooms in one state, and a stated need.
