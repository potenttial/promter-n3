Cruising from Puerto Vallarta: what US and Canadian travelers should know about Mexico's first home port

Puerto Vallarta will become Mexico's first cruise home port, where voyages start and end. What's confirmed, what isn't, and how to plan a fly-and-cruise trip from the US or Canada.

By Travelytix Editors · November 18, 2026 · 1 min read

Today most cruise passengers see Puerto Vallarta for a few hours on a port day. Mexico now plans to change that: Puerto Vallarta will become the country’s first cruise home port, meaning voyages will start and end there.

For travelers in the US and Canada, that opens a new trip format: fly to Vallarta, spend a few days, then sail.

How we’d plan a fly-and-cruise trip

  1. Wait for the schedule. Don’t book flights until a cruise line confirms departures from the new terminal.
  2. Arrive at least one day early. Flight delays shouldn’t put your sailing at risk.
  3. Add a night after. Disembarking day is a good time to enjoy the malecón or the Zona Romántica.
  4. Budget the extra hotel nights. That’s the main cost difference from a regular port-of-call stop.

Bottom line: great news for West Coast and Canadian travelers who want Mexican Pacific cruises with a beach stay attached. But there’s nothing to book yet.

Why Mexico is investing

From January to July 2026, Mexican ports welcomed 7.5 million cruise passengers (+16.2% year over year) who spent US$648.9 million.

FAQ

Can I book a cruise that departs from Puerto Vallarta's new terminal now?

Not yet. Mexico hasn't announced an opening date or which cruise lines will use the new home port. Wait for official schedules before booking flights around it.

What documents do US and Canadian citizens need?

To fly to Mexico you need a valid passport. Check your cruise line's document rules too, since requirements can differ for cruises that start outside the US.

How big will the terminal be?

About 95,172 m² (roughly 1.02 million sq ft) of land and water, with three piers.
